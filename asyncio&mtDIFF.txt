Asyncio and Multithreading both help Python handle multiple tasks without waiting for every task to finish one by one.
Imagine this situation
You are at home. You have three tasks:
1. Washing clothes.
2. Preparing breakfast.
3. Cleaning your room.
You don't want to wait for washing clothes to finish before starting breakfast.

So, how can you manage these tasks?
2. Multithreading – Taking help from multiple people
Imagine you have three people at home.

- Person 1 washes clothes.
- Person 2 prepares breakfast.
- Person 3 cleans the room.
All three people work on different tasks at the same time.

This is the basic idea of Multithreading.

In Python:
- Each person represents a thread.
- Each thread performs a task.
- Multiple threads can make progress concurrently.



Asyncio – One person managing multiple tasks
Now imagine you are alone.
You start washing clothes.

While the washing machine is running, you don't need to stand there and wait.

You start preparing breakfast.

While the food is cooking, you start cleaning the room.

Whenever one task is waiting, you move to another task.

This is the basic idea of Asyncio.

In Python:
- You generally have one thread.
- An event loop manages different tasks.
- When one task waits, another task gets a chance to run.
- await is used to pause a coroutine while it waits.
